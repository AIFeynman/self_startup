Subject + action + camera + lighting + sound

22 of the 53 ai animation pages i went through have a reel over 10M views. the ones with the biggest numbers share a few habits you could copy in a weekend: two cartoon characters, a caption that asks you something and a posting time that doesn't move.

from the outside every page looks the same: a cute character, warm lighting, soft music. so people copy the look, post reel after reel and wonder why theirs stall. the look is the easy part. it's also the part everyone already has.
i read the first 12 reels on every page's Reels tab on oct 24 and 25, then pulled post times and captions for the pages that stood out. this is the full breakdown: what kind of page wins, how much one reel carries, the 4 caption formulas, the team running 6 pages on one schedule, when to post and what i'd do if i started a page this week.
the dataset
53 english ai video pages: cartoons, 3d family stories, cozy rooms and asmr worlds. i found them by starting from pages that already had big reels and following instagram's "accounts you may like" one or two steps out.
for each page i took the first 12 reels on the Reels tab and their view counts, 636 reels in total. 38 of the pages have at least one reel over 1M and 22 have one over 10M. pinned reels count too, because that's what a visitor sees first.

two characters beat one world
i sorted every page into three groups: two recurring characters (usually a couple), one recurring character, or no character at all (rooms, worlds, asmr).
![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVcHGDRW4AAJRob.jpg)
the middle reel on a two-character page sits at 112.8K views, almost level with one-character pages at 110K, while pages with no character at all sit at 30.2K. that's about 3.7x more views for giving people someone to come back to.
two-character pages are also the biggest group, 20 of the 53: bears, otters, penguins, capybaras, squirrels and one married couple. 10 of those 20 have a reel over 10M.

the lookalike pages prove it too. the otter couple page @ottiandollo has a middle reel of 1.78M. a page with almost the same name and the same animal has a middle reel of 117K. people come back for the characters. the animal alone doesn't carry it.
![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVcJw0DWsAA3SfW.jpg)
one reel carries the page
on the median page, the biggest of its 12 reels holds 45% of all 12 reels' views. on 21 of the 53 pages it holds more than half.

that's why so many of them pin their hit. @hank_hancy pins a 182M reel and a 43.5M reel above daily posts that mostly land between 20K and 2M. a new visitor sees the 182M first, so that one number sells the whole page.
![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVcMPH7XMAE1ogR.jpg)
for a new page this changes the job. you can't know which reel will be the one, so you make enough reels in the same shape that one of them gets the chance.
the caption does more work than you think
the pages with the steadiest numbers write the same kind of caption every time. four caption formulas kept showing up.
![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVcHI1WW0AA_8rM.jpg)

![](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/HVcHKxPXUAA4SZB.jpg)
